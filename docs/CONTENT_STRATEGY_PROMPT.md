## Voice & Tone

### Core Principle
Founders are making high-stakes decisions with imperfect information. This content helps them understand what matters, what doesn't, and how to move forward without wasting time or money.

### Voice Characteristics
- **Direct** - No throat-clearing, no hedge-word soup
- **Practical** - Actionable guidance, not just concepts
- **Stakes-aware** - Understands what's at risk
- **Confident but honest** - Clear about what we know and what depends

### What to Avoid
- ❌ Academic tone ("pursuant to," "hereinafter")
- ❌ Excessive hedging ("may," "could," "potentially" every sentence)
- ❌ Generic AI slop (overused fonts, purple gradients, buzzwords)
- ❌ False simplification ("just do X" when there are trade-offs)

### What to Use
- ✅ Clear stakes framing ("Miss this deadline and...")
- ✅ Concrete examples with real numbers
- ✅ Direct instruction ("File within 30 days")
- ✅ Honest trade-offs ("This is simpler but less flexible")

## Content Types

### 1. Core Guides (1,500-2,200 words, 10-15 min read)
**Purpose**: Comprehensive treatment of foundational topics
**When to use**: Concepts every founder needs to understand deeply

**Structure**:
- Stakes-aware intro (what's at risk)
- Core concept explanation
- 2-3 worked examples with numbers
- Common mistakes section
- "How We Help" (specific services)
- Next steps / related reading

**Examples**:
- Incorporating Your Startup: Delaware C-Corps
- SAFEs: The Complete Guide
- Founder Equity: Stock, Vesting, and 83(b) Elections

### 2. Deep Dives (900-1,400 words, 6-9 min read)
**Purpose**: Focused exploration of specific mechanisms or decisions
**When to use**: Complex nuances that deserve dedicated treatment

**Structure**:
- Direct problem statement
- Technical explanation
- Side-by-side comparison or scenario walkthrough
- When this matters (and when it doesn't)
- "How We Help" (brief)

**Examples**:
- Post-Money vs Pre-Money SAFEs: What Changes
- Single vs Double-Trigger Acceleration
- Qualified Small Business Stock (QSBS) Explained

### 3. FAQs (300-800 words, 3-5 min read)
**Purpose**: Quick reference for common questions
**When to use**: Simple yes/no or "here's what you need" questions

**Structure**:
- Direct answer up front
- Brief explanation
- Key deadline or action item
- Link to related deep dive

**Examples**:
- Do I Need an 83(b) Election?
- SAFE vs Convertible Note: Which One?
- When Do I Need a 409A Valuation?

## Structural Requirements

### Every Post Must Have

1. **Opening Stakes**
   - First 1-2 paragraphs
   - What happens if you get this wrong
   - Why reading this matters right now

2. **Scannable Sections**
   - Clear H2/H3 hierarchy
   - Short paragraphs (2-3 sentences)
   - Bullet points for lists
   - Visual breaks with horizontal rules

3. **Concrete Examples**
   - Real numbers ("$1M on a $10M cap")
   - Specific timelines ("within 30 days")
   - Named scenarios ("Series A at $15M pre")

4. **"How We Help" Section**
   - Specific service descriptions
   - No marketing jargon
   - Clear scope of what we actually do
   - Not sales-y (informative, not persuasive)

5. **Legal Disclaimer**
   - Small text at bottom
   - Standard "for informational purposes" language

### What NOT to Include

- ❌ Long theoretical introductions
- ❌ "In this post we'll cover..." meta-text
- ❌ Exhaustive citations or footnotes
- ❌ Hedge-word stacking ("might potentially possibly")
- ❌ Generic templates without customization

## Writing Rules

### Paragraph Length
- **Maximum 3 sentences per paragraph**
- One idea per paragraph
- Use white space generously
- Break up long blocks of text

### Sentence Structure
- **Active voice preferred**
  - ✅ "You file the election"
  - ❌ "The election is filed"

- **Front-load key information**
  - ✅ "File within 30 days or you lose the benefit"
  - ❌ "You should be aware that there is a deadline"

- **Vary sentence length** for rhythm
  - Mix short punchy sentences with longer explanatory ones

### Word Choice
- **Use "you" not "founders" or "companies"**
  - Makes it personal and direct

- **Prefer simple words**
  - ✅ "use" not "utilize"
  - ✅ "end" not "terminate"
  - ✅ "get" not "obtain"

- **Be specific with numbers**
  - ✅ "$5M to $10M" not "millions of dollars"
  - ✅ "30 days" not "about a month"
  - ✅ "20%" not "significant percentage"

### Formatting
- **Bold for emphasis** - Use sparingly for critical points
- **Italics for examples** - "e.g., restricted stock"
- **Lists for parallel items** - Bullets or numbers
- **Tables for comparisons** - SAFE vs Note, Pre vs Post
- **Code/highlight boxes** for warnings or key takeaways

## Example Openings

### ✅ Good - Direct Stakes
> You have 30 days from receiving restricted stock to file an 83(b) election. Miss that deadline and you could owe taxes on hundreds of thousands of dollars of paper gains you can't sell. The form itself is one page. The consequence of not filing it can be devastating.

### ✅ Good - Problem Framing
> Delaware C-corp is the standard structure for VC-backed startups. Nearly every institutional investor expects it. Your incorporation structure isn't a detail to figure out later—it's foundational, and switching later creates tax headaches and legal complexity you don't want.

### ❌ Bad - Generic Opening
> In this comprehensive guide, we'll walk you through everything you need to know about SAFEs, including the history, various types, and when to use them. By the end of this post, you'll have a complete understanding of this important fundraising tool.

### ❌ Bad - Too Academic
> Pursuant to Section 83(b) of the Internal Revenue Code, taxpayers who acquire property in connection with the performance of services may elect to include the value of such property in gross income at the time of transfer rather than at the time of vesting.

## "How We Help" Section Guidelines

### Purpose
Show specific, practical ways SFP helps with the topic at hand. Not sales copy—service description.

### Format
Brief intro + bulleted list of 2-4 services, each with:
- Service name (bold)
- What it includes (1-2 sentences)

### Tone
- Informative, not persuasive
- Specific scope, not vague promises
- "We draft SAFEs" not "We leverage synergies"

### Example

**Good**:
> We handle SAFE rounds for early-stage startups:
> - **SAFE preparation** - Drafting based on Y Combinator templates, customizing terms, cap modeling
> - **Round management** - Coordinating multiple investors, tracking signatures and wires, updating cap table
> - **Conversion support** - Series A conversion mechanics, calculating share counts, coordinating with counsel

**Bad**:
> At SFP, we're passionate about helping founders succeed. Our team of experienced attorneys leverages deep expertise to provide world-class legal services tailored to your unique needs. Contact us today to learn more about how we can help you achieve your goals.
